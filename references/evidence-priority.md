# Evidence priority

## Source tiers

### Tier A: direct and auditable

Regulatory filings, audited statements, official permits, procurement records, standards, official technical documentation, dated company releases, and primary transaction records. Use these for identity, timing, legal status, capacity, revenue, shares, and reported operations.

### Tier B: close-to-event corroboration

Customer or supplier disclosures, conference presentations, engineering papers, facility records, job postings, shipping or trade records, and credible specialist reporting. Use these to triangulate qualification, ramp, yield, allocation, and route alternatives.

### Tier C: analytical interpretation

Industry research, broker estimates, market commentary, and expert interviews. Use these for hypotheses and expectation mapping, not as sole proof of a material edge.

### Tier D: discovery only

Discussion boards, short-form posts, unverified screenshots, unattributed charts, and reposts. They can generate search terms or countercases but should not independently support a material conclusion.

## Independence

An independence group is the economic or editorial origin that could share a bias. Five articles repeating one press release count as one group. Record the group explicitly and seek a second group before promoting a fragile claim.

## Citation discipline

Every material number needs units, currency, date, method, and locator. Cite the smallest source span that establishes the claim. Do not copy source prose into the repository. For restricted material, store only a stable ID, hash, metadata, and access level in the public overlay.

## Source conflict

When sources disagree:

1. preserve both records;
2. check the clocks and definitions;
3. compare primary versus derivative status;
4. identify whether the disagreement is scope, timing, accounting, or fact;
5. mark the claim CONTRADICTED or UNKNOWN until resolved.
